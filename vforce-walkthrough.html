VForce360: a governed lakehouse with a natural language analyst

A single continuous walkthrough of VForce360, the governed data lakehouse Edgent LLC builds and operates. Most of it was recorded on open demonstration tenants with no sign-in, so those screens can be reproduced by anyone who opens the same link and types the same thing. Two segments are recorded signed in, and the narration says so at the point it happens. The data is synthetic.

Narrated and captioned. Captions are burned into a band below the picture, so they never cover the screen being discussed, and a WebVTT text track is available in the player caption control. Download the video (MP4) Download captions (WebVTT) Download captions (SRT)

What the walkthrough shows

The sequence follows the platform the way an evaluator would work through it: find the data, ask it a question, check that the answer is traceable, then confirm that the parts a buyer has to defend, authorization, human review and provenance, are real controls rather than claims.

ChapterWhat it covers
The catalogOne tenant, four catalogs. 29 curated gold tables, 21 conformed silver tables behind them, and a catalog of pages produced by the document pipeline. Medallion layers and the audit catalog are first class objects, not folders.
One table, in fullColumns, types, per-column quality status and real sample rows on a chronic-absenteeism table, including the suppressed flag that decides whether a rate may be published at all.
Where the table came fromLineage is derived, not drawn. Seven tables and seven edges, each resolving to the run, transform or audit record that produced it.
Quality rules on that tableFour scored checks declared on the table itself, including a minimum cell size rule, each with a severity and a run history. Across the catalog, 20 rules over 7 tables at 99.3 percent, and the one rule sitting at 85.9 percent is shown rather than hidden.
Ask the data a questionPlain language to governed SQL. The answer arrives as rows and the SQL is shown. Nine of the ten rows come back masked, because the per-school groups are below the minimum reporting size; only the district total is released.
The rule is in the platformThe same query, rewritten by hand in the SQL editor, returns the same masked cells. Suppression is enforced on the read path, not chosen by the analyst.
Access controlThe same object, two subjects. Anonymous is refused with the missing relation named. Signed in, the grants are listed. The decision engine is then asked directly and answers for both subjects, with the grant behind each verdict written out, and the compliance ledger shows the allowances and refusals as hash-chained records.
Documents285 scanned pages across 18 batches, read, classified, summarized and scored, with 80 pages queued for a person and the reason each one landed there recorded next to it.
Building a pipelineA four-step pipeline built on screen: pull an extract, normalize rows, a quality gate that reads the rejected row count, and publish to the lakehouse. Named, saved, versioned and attributed in the audit trail.
A pipeline in productionA live graph read from the engine, with a real conditional branch and its run history.
Published to the publicA school district transparency site on the same platform. Attendance charted against labeled axes and compared year over year, the whole page narrowed to one school, and small-population suppression that names exactly which schools, grades and measures were withheld rather than leaving a blank.

What this recording is, and is not

  • Edgent built and operates this. VForce360 is Edgent's own product, running on Edgent's own infrastructure. It is not a reseller demonstration and it is not a mockup.
  • The data is synthetic. The district figures, the student records and the scanned pages are generated or public-domain material used for demonstration. No real student, patient or claimant data appears anywhere in the recording or on the tenants.
  • Nothing was staged or sped up. The walkthrough was captured by driving a real browser against live tenants. Page loads were trimmed at the head of each shot so the video is not padded with spinners, and the pipeline-building segment is cut between two beats of one continuous take. No result, latency or screen was altered, and no segment is played faster than it happened.
  • Where sign-in is used, and why. Three segments are recorded signed in, because they cannot honestly be shown otherwise: the granted half of the access-control comparison, the decision-engine and ledger views behind it, and the pipeline being authored, which is a write that the public tenant deliberately refuses. The narration states this at the point it happens. Everything else needs no account.
  • Charts were chosen for whether they are readable. Where a chart on a demonstration tenant renders without a usable axis or with every bar at the same length, it is not in this video. The published district site is used for the charting segment because its axes are labeled and its values are printed.
  • Accessibility. Captions are burned into a dedicated band below the picture, so no caption ever covers the interface being described, and the delivered frame is 1920 by 1200 for that reason. WebVTT and SRT are downloadable above, and the WebVTT carries line positioning so a player that draws its own captions puts them in the band.
